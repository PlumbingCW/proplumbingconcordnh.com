1.

Winter in Hooksett: freeze-ups, thaws and backups.

New Hampshire winters are the busiest time for emergency plumbing in Hooksett. Pipes freeze in garages and exterior walls, split, and then leak when the weather warms. Sewer lines carry the thaw load too, as melting snow and rain push groundwater into older systems.

If a pipe has frozen, open the nearest tap, warm it gently with a hair dryer and call us if you cannot reach it. If it has split, shut the main. See burst pipe repair for what we do next.

2.

Water and sewer billing in Hooksett.

Water in parts of Hooksett comes from the Central Hooksett Water Precinct, formed in 1956, which buys all of its water from Manchester Water Works, drawn from Lake Massabesic. It serves areas including Granite Hill, Granite Heights, Campbell Hill Estates, Glencrest Estates and Berry Hill Estates, and a separate Hooksett Village Water system also exists. The precinct office is at 10 Water Works Drive, 603-624-0608.

Sewer is billed quarterly by the Hooksett Sewer Commission. The commission's page says homes that use a private well but connect to sewer are charged for 20,000 gallons a quarter unless a well meter is installed. If that is you, a well meter is a plumbing job we can do, and it can reduce the charge when your real use is lower.

3.

A leak and a sewer bill.

Water that leaks outside the house, such as a broken irrigation line or a toilet that runs, can raise a metered bill. Water that does not reach the sewer, such as a garden hose, is still billed in some systems. Ask the precinct or the commission how your account handles it, and check your meter before you call. Our leak detection page explains how.

A meter that moves with every tap closed is the quickest sign. We find the source, repair it and give you notes you can share with the billing office.

4.

The I-93 corridor and the villages.

Hooksett spans several villages: Hooksett itself, Martins Corner, Rowes Corner and South Hooksett. US 3 and NH 28, known locally as Hooksett Road and the Daniel Webster Highway, and NH 3A along West River Road, carry most of the traffic, with I-93 interchanges at NH 28, NH 3A and Hackett Hill Road.

Robie's Country Store, a National Historic Landmark, is a handy way to describe where you are if the dispatcher asks. We run both residential and small commercial calls here, and our commercial plumbing team covers shops along the highway.

5.

Housing mix and what it means for plumbing.

Hooksett's roughly 14,900 residents live in a mix of single-family homes in subdivisions and older homes near the villages. Subdivision homes on precinct water tend to have simpler, newer plumbing. Older homes need a closer look at galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains and old water heaters.

If you are thinking about a planned job, water heater installation is a good example. We size the new tank, check venting and handle the permit step.

6.

Before the plumber comes to Hooksett.

  • Know your water. Precinct supply or private well.
  • Know your sewer. Town sewer or septic.
  • Find the shutoff. The main valve and the one at the water heater.
  • Check the meter. A moving dial with taps closed means a leak.
  • Clear access. Basement stairs and the heater area.
  • Have the bill handy. It helps if a water or sewer account is involved.
7.

Small businesses along the highway.

Along US 3 and NH 28, shops, offices and restaurants have plumbing calls that cannot wait. A restroom backup or a failed water heater stops a business in its tracks. We take after-hours calls and plan the permanent repair around your opening hours.
