Sewer Camera Inspection in Concord, NH.

A sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside a drain line without digging, which makes it the best first step before a home purchase or after repeated clogs. We run inspections in Concord with video you can keep and a locator that marks problems on the surface.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

What a sewer camera inspection finds.

A waterproof camera on a flexible cable goes through the cleanout and down the line, sending live video to a screen. It shows root intrusion, cracks, offset joints, bellies where the pipe sags and holds water, scale on cast iron and any sections that have collapsed.

A locator on the surface follows the camera head, so we can tell you how deep the pipe is and mark the spot of any defect. That turns a vague problem into a precise one that can be repaired with a short dig.

2.

When to schedule an inspection.

Common reasons are buying a house, especially an older one, repeated backups, a slow drain that returns after cleaning, a sewer smell and plans to build over or near the line. A pre-purchase inspection costs a small fraction of a surprise sewer repair, and it gives you a negotiating position.

Concord has a mix of housing ages, and homes built before plastic pipe became standard often have cast iron or clay laterals. A camera tells you which you have and in what condition.

3.

What you get after the visit.

Deliverables.

  • 1.A video file of the inspection.
  • 2.The pipe material, size and approximate depth.
  • 3.The location of any defect marked on the surface.
  • 4.A plain-language summary of findings.
  • 5.Repair options if anything needs fixing.

If the camera shows a problem, we do not push repair. We explain the options, and you can take the video to another contractor if you prefer.

4.

Home buyers and sellers.

A standard home inspection does not look inside the sewer line. For a house with large trees, an old foundation or a history of backups, the camera is worth the cost. Sellers use inspections to find and fix problems before listing, which avoids last-minute surprises.

If you are buying in Bow, Hooksett or Pembroke, tell us the address. Homes on town sewer can be inspected the same way, and homes on septic need a different kind of inspection. Our Hooksett page explains how sewer billing and connection work there.

5.

Limits of a camera.

A camera cannot go through a full blockage or a severe collapse. If the line is blocked, we clear it first. A very small pipe or a sharp bend can stop the cable, and we say so rather than guess.

We also do not certify a line for a lender or the city. We report what we see. If a repair is needed after a drain cleaning, we show you the footage that supports it.

6.

Reading the footage: what common defects mean.

  • Root intrusion. Fine roots entering at joints. A cut and a repair or liner stops it from returning.
  • Offset joint. Two pipe sections that no longer line up. Debris catches here, and the pipe may need a spot repair.
  • Belly. A low spot where water pools. It collects solids and causes repeat clogs, and may need the section replaced.
  • Scale and tuberculation. Rough buildup in cast iron. Cleaning helps for a while, but heavy scale means the pipe is aging out.
  • Crack or break. Soil and roots enter and sewage leaks out. Repair is needed, and soil washing in can undermine the ground above.

You do not need to read it yourself. We pause on each defect, explain what it is and tell you how urgent it is.

7.

Properties on septic or well systems.

Not every home near Concord is on city sewer. Bow and Loudon homes are mostly on private septic. A camera can check the pipe between the house and the tank, which is where many problems start, but it does not tell you the condition of the tank or the leach field.

If you are buying such a house, plan on a separate septic inspection by a specialist alongside our line inspection. We will tell you which parts of the system our camera can and cannot see.
