Emergency Hydro Jetting in Concord, NH.

When a line backs up for the third time in a year, cabling alone is not working, and hydro jetting is the next step. Our crews run jetting calls 24/7 in Concord. Until we arrive, stop running water in the house and keep drains closed.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

When a backup calls for jetting right away.

A hydro jetter uses pressurized water from a nozzle that pulls itself down the pipe, scouring the walls as it goes. When a main line is full of grease, silt or packed scale and a cable only punches a hole, jetting restores the full bore of the pipe. It is the better answer when the line has backed up repeatedly.

If sewage is on the floor now, we may clear it first with a cable to get the house usable, and then jet to prevent a repeat. You do not have to decide while the house is flooded.

2.

Jetting versus cabling.

How the two methods compare.
CablingHydro jetting.
What it doesPunches or cuts through a blockageScours the whole pipe wall.
Best forSingle clogs, roots at one spotGrease, scale, silt, repeat backups.
Leaves behindCoating on pipe wallsA clean pipe.
Not suited forHeavy grease coatingCollapsed or badly cracked pipe.

Roots are cut with a cable first, and jetting follows to wash out the debris. For everyday sink and tub clogs, the simpler tools described on our drain cleaning page are enough.

3.

Where jetting pays off.

Kitchen lines in homes with heavy cooking, restaurants and apartment buildings with shared stacks collect grease year after year. A jet clears the grease from the pipe wall, not just the middle. Apartment and multi-family buildings, which make up a large share of Concord's housing, benefit most.

Old cast iron lines with scale can also be cleaned with jetting, though the scale sometimes covers a crack, so we run a camera before and after. We will not jet a pipe that the camera shows is collapsing.

Hydro Jetting help is one call away, any hour of the day.

4.

What happens during a jetting visit.

  1. Camera first. We look at the pipe to choose a safe pressure and nozzle.
  2. Set up at the cleanout. The hose goes in through the cleanout or a removed toilet.
  3. Jet toward the street. We push forward, then pull back slowly to scour the walls.
  4. Camera after. We confirm the pipe is clean and check for damage the debris hid.
  5. Report. You see the footage and hear whether the line needs follow-up.
5.

Cost drivers and how often to repeat.

Price depends on pipe length, access, the amount of buildup and how many passes the line needs. We give you a number before work starts.

A line that was jetted needs less frequent service. Grease lines in commercial kitchens may need it on a schedule, and a clean residential line may never need it again. We recommend an interval based on what we found, not on a calendar. See sewer line cleaning for main line work.

6.

Shared stacks and older buildings.

Concord's housing includes thousands of two-to-four unit buildings and larger apartment buildings, and Penacook still has mill-era construction. In those buildings, one kitchen stack can serve several apartments, and grease from every unit coats the same pipe. A backup in the ground-floor unit is often a problem created upstairs.

For a landlord, jetting a shared line once and then setting a cleaning interval is cheaper than answering repeated emergency calls. We camera the line, show you the buildup and recommend a schedule that fits what we saw.

7.

What jetting will not fix.

Jetting clears debris. It does not repair a crack, straighten an offset joint or lift a belly in the pipe. If the camera shows that a line holds standing water or is breaking down, we tell you that jetting will give temporary relief and a repair is the real answer. Our sewer line repair page explains those options.

Before the jetting crew arrives.

  • 1.Clear the area around the cleanout.
  • 2.Locate the main cleanout and tell us where it is.
  • 3.Tell us about any previous cabling and when it happened.
  • 4.Move stored items away from floor drains.
  • 5.Keep water use off until we say it is safe.
