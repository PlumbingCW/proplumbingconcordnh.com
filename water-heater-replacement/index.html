Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Concord, NH.

A tank that is leaking onto the floor or has stopped making hot water is an emergency in a Concord winter, so our dispatch line answers 24/7 for replacement calls. While you wait, shut off the cold water supply valve above the heater, switch off the power or set a gas unit to pilot, and keep the area clear.

1.

When a failed water heater is an emergency.

Water pooling around the base of a tank is the clearest sign that it is finished. A steel tank corrodes from the inside, and once the liner lets go there is no patch that lasts. The leak usually starts small and then opens up, which is why a damp spot on Monday can be a flooded utility room by Friday.

No hot water at all is the other common trigger. In a house with kids, a long cold stretch in January, or a business that needs hot water to operate, same-day replacement matters. We schedule same-day appointments when available and tell you plainly when a unit has to be ordered.

A tank that rumbles, runs rusty hot water or trips its relief valve repeatedly is on borrowed time. Those are not middle-of-the-night problems, but they are worth a call before the tank picks its own moment to fail.

2.

Repair or replace: a quick decision guide.

Age and symptom decide most cases. A leak from the tank body means replacement. A failed thermostat, element, igniter or thermocouple on a younger unit usually means repair, which we cover on our water heater repair page.

3.

Choosing the replacement.

Most Concord homes get a like-for-like swap, a 40 or 50 gallon tank in either gas or electric. Matching fuel and size keeps the job short and the price lower. If your household has grown or shrunk, we will size the new unit to how you actually use hot water.

If you want to stop heating a tank all day, a tankless water heater is the main alternative. It costs more up front, needs the right gas line or electrical capacity, and pays back slowly. We explain both so you can compare real numbers.

Hybrid heat pump and electric options.

A hybrid unit pulls heat from the surrounding air and uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. It needs open space and a drain for condensate, and it cools the room it sits in. In a New Hampshire basement that is often a fair trade, but the install needs a good look first.

4.

How soft Concord water treats a new tank.

Concord's treated water measured about 12 parts per million of hardness in the city's 2022 report, which is very soft. Scale is rarely what kills a water heater here. Steady corrosion of the steel shell is, along with an anode rod that wore out years ago.

The city also treats its water with chloramine and adjusts pH for corrosion control. We replace the sacrificial anode rod on tanks we service and tell you when it is worth checking again. That small habit is the cheapest way to add years to a tank.

Homes in Bow, Loudon and other private-well areas are different. Well water can carry iron, manganese or hardness that shortens heater life. Our Bow plumbing page covers what well owners should know.

5.

What a replacement visit includes.

  1. Shut off and drain. We close the supply, kill power or gas to the old unit and drain the tank safely.
  2. Remove and haul away. The old heater leaves with us, so you are not left with a 150 pound tank.
  3. Set the new unit. New flex connectors, dielectric fittings where metals meet, a proper relief valve and discharge line.
  4. Vent and test. Gas units get a venting check and a combustion test. Electric units get a voltage and element check.
  5. Explain the settings. We set the thermostat to a safe temperature and show you the shutoff and drain points.

Whether a permit is needed depends on the work. Where one applies, the inspection is scheduled by the licensed contractor with the city's plumbing inspector through the Building Safety Division on Green Street, and we handle that step.

6.

What drives the price.

We give you the price before work starts, based on what we see at your house. These are the factors that move it most.

  • Tank size and fuel. A 50 gallon gas unit costs more than a 40 gallon electric one, and a tankless unit costs more again.
  • Venting and gas piping. Changing from an old flue to new venting, or running a larger gas line, adds labor.
  • Code updates. Expansion tanks, seismic-style strapping where required, and drain pans are added when the setup calls for them.
  • Access. A heater in a tight crawlspace or up a narrow basement stair takes longer to swap.
  • Timing. After-hours emergency work can be priced differently from a scheduled daytime visit.
7.

Before the plumber arrives.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

  • 1.Turn off the cold supply valve on top of the tank.
  • 2.Switch off the breaker or set the gas valve to pilot.
  • 3.Move anything stored near the heater.
  • 4.Clear a path from the basement door to the unit.
  • 5.Note the tank label: size, fuel and age if shown.
