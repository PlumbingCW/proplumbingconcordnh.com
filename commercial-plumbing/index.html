Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Concord, NH.

A plumbing failure in a business costs money by the hour, so our line is open 24/7 for commercial calls in Concord. While you wait, shut off the water to the affected area, keep customers and staff away from wet floors and note which fixtures are down.

1.

After-hours plumbing emergencies in a business.

A restaurant with a backed-up kitchen drain cannot serve food. An office with a burst supply line has water in the ceiling over computers. A store with no hot water or a flooded restroom can lose customers. We prioritize getting the problem contained fast, and then plan the permanent fix around your hours.

Tell the dispatcher the building type, how many people are inside, where the main shutoff is and whether you are the owner or a tenant. If you are a tenant, we can coordinate with the property manager.

What to do before help arrives.

  • 1.Shut off water to the affected area or the main.
  • 2.Keep staff and customers off wet floors.
  • 3.Cover or move equipment and stock away from the water.
  • 4.Switch off power to wet areas only if it is safe to reach the panel.
  • 5.Photograph the damage for the owner and insurer.
2.

What we handle for small businesses.

We work in storefronts, offices, small restaurants, salons, rentals and multi-unit buildings, including the older mixed-use buildings downtown. Typical work includes leak repair, drain and sewer cleaning, water heater service, fixture repair, restroom upgrades and pipe replacement.

We do not take on large new-construction or multi-story mechanical projects. For repair, maintenance and tenant fit-out work in small and mid-size buildings, we are set up and ready.

3.

Leaks in commercial buildings.

A leak in a building has more questions than a house leak. Whose pipe is it, who pays and how do you keep the space open? We isolate the section, locate the source with leak detection gear and give you a written note describing where it started.

Some commercial leak calls end in a no, when a property owner decides to wait or a tenant disputes who is responsible. We give the facts and the options, and the decision stays with you.

Commercial Plumbing help is one call away, any hour of the day.

4.

Backflow testing.

A backflow preventer is a device that keeps dirty water from flowing back into the clean supply. Many commercial properties, as well as irrigation systems, must have these devices tested on a schedule. The test confirms that the check valves hold.

Testing is a planned service, not an emergency. We test and report the results, and we repair or replace a device that fails. Ask us which schedule your property needs, since requirements depend on the water supplier and the device.

5.

Grease traps and storm drains.

Restaurants and food businesses use grease traps or interceptors to keep fats out of the sewer. They need regular cleaning, or grease passes through and clogs the line. We service small commercial grease traps and flag when a larger trap needs a specialist hauler.

Storm drains and catch basins fill with silt and leaves. Cleaning them before a heavy rain prevents ponding in a parking lot. We handle small property drains and can jet them to clear debris. For sanitary lines, see sewer line cleaning.

6.

Planning maintenance.

A simple maintenance list for owners.

  • 1.Know where every shutoff valve is and label it.
  • 2.Test the main shutoff twice a year.
  • 3.Schedule grease trap cleaning on a calendar.
  • 4.Have backflow devices tested as required.
  • 5.Replace old water heaters before they fail.

A short walk-through with a plumber finds the weak points before they become after-hours calls. We tell you what we see and what is urgent.

7.

Restrooms, water heaters and tenant fit-outs.

A restroom with a running toilet, a slow drain or a leaking faucet is a customer-facing problem, and we handle these with the same-day appointments when available. For tenant fit-outs we add fixtures, run new lines and coordinate the work around your open hours.

Small businesses often rely on a single tank water heater that has been running for years. When it fails during a busy week, the cost is not the unit, it is the lost day. We recommend replacing an old commercial heater on your schedule, using the guidance on our water heater replacement page.
