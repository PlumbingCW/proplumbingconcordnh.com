Water Heater Control Valve Replacement for Concord, NH Homes.

A water heater has several valves, and they fail in different ways. This guide explains the gas control valve, the temperature and pressure relief valve and the drain valve, so you can tell what is wrong before a plumber arrives.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

Three valves, three jobs.

The gas control valve, or thermostat valve on a gas unit, regulates the burner and holds the pilot. The temperature and pressure relief valve opens if the tank gets too hot or pressure builds too high. The drain valve at the base lets you flush the tank. A leak or malfunction in any of them has a different cause and a different fix.

Replacing the right one is the difference between a quick repair and a call about a new tank. Do not guess which valve is leaking before you look.

2.

Gas control valve problems.

Symptoms of a failing gas control include a pilot that will not stay lit, a burner that does not fire even though the pilot is on, water that is too hot or too cold regardless of the setting and a clicking that never produces flame. On a younger tank, replacing the control valve is usually cheaper than replacing the tank. On an older tank, it may not be worth it.

Gas work is safety work. If you smell gas, leave the house, call 911 or your gas utility from outside and do not use switches. Do not work on a gas valve yourself. We will not relight a heater that shows back-drafting or soot until the venting is sound. See our water heater repair page.

3.

The relief valve.

A relief valve that drips or discharges is doing its job, or it is worn. If it discharges after the heater runs, thermal expansion is probably spiking pressure, which an expansion tank can fix. If it drips constantly at normal pressure, the valve seat is worn and the valve should be replaced.

Never cap or plug a relief valve. It is the last safety device before a tank over-pressurizes. Replace it with the same pressure and temperature rating, and keep the discharge line pointed down, ending near the floor.

4.

The drain valve.

A plastic drain valve that leaks a slow drip is common on older tanks. Replacing it with a brass valve is a good upgrade. To change it, you need to drain the tank first, which takes time. A garden-hose cap on the end is a stopgap that works for a while.

If the tank has not been flushed in years, sediment may block the valve. We flush it, replace the valve and tell you how the tank looks inside.

5.

Repair the valve or replace the tank.

SituationCommon choice.

A valve on a failing tank is money spent on borrowed time. If your heater is old, ask for both prices. Our replacement page covers the full job.

6.

Testing the relief valve safely.

Once a year, lift the lever on the relief valve briefly with a bucket under the discharge line. Water should flow and stop when you release it. If it keeps dripping, the seat has debris or is worn, and the valve should be replaced. If it does not flow at all, it is stuck, which is worse, because it cannot protect the tank.

Be careful. The water can be scalding. Let the heater cool if you can, and keep your hands clear of the discharge line. If you are unsure, a plumber can test it during a visit.

7.

Signs the valve problem is really a tank problem.

Sometimes a valve fails because the tank behind it is failing. Rust-colored water, a rumbling sound from sediment, a tank that is more than a decade old and water pooling under the heater are all signs that the shell is going. In those cases, a new valve fixes one symptom and leaves the cause.

A quick way to tell is to look at the base of the tank. Rust streaks, wet insulation and corrosion on the bottom seam suggest an end of life. We will tell you honestly whether the part you are replacing will be outlived by the tank around it, and give you the repair price next to the replacement price.

8.

Safety checks after any valve work.

After a valve is replaced, the work is not finished until it is tested. For a gas control, that means a leak check at every joint with a bubble solution, a look at the flame and a check that the burner lights smoothly and shuts off when the tank is hot. For a relief valve, it means confirming that the discharge line is intact and pointed down. For a drain valve, it means checking for weeping after the tank refills.

Ask whoever did the work to show you each check. A plumber who tests in front of you is telling you the job is done. Keep the paperwork, note the date and mark your calendar to look at the relief valve next year. Small habits like that are what stop a minor valve issue from becoming a failed tank.

9.

Local note for Concord homes.

Local note.

Soft water, steel tanks.

The City of Concord reported 2022 distribution hardness of about 12 parts per million, which is soft. Valves and heating elements in Concord homes tend to suffer less from scale than those in hard-water areas, so when a control valve fails here, age and corrosion are the more likely causes than mineral buildup.

In Hooksett, homes on the Central Hooksett Water Precinct get water bought from Manchester Water Works, and homes on private wells in Pembroke and Loudon see different water. Our Hooksett and Loudon pages explain how the water source changes the advice.
