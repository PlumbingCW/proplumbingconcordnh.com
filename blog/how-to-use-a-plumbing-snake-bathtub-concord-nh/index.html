How to Use a Plumbing Snake in a Bathtub: Concord, NH Guide.

A slow tub is usually hair and soap, and a hand snake can clear it in minutes if you know where to put it. Here is how to do it without scratching the tub or pushing the clog deeper.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

Where tub clogs hide.

A tub drains through the stopper or strainer, a short tailpiece, a P-trap and then the branch line. Nine times in ten, the clog is a mat of hair and soap in the first foot below the drain, or in the trap. Overflow plates are a second path, and many tubs can be snaked through them.

Pull the stopper first. Lift or unscrew it and clean off the hair. A lot of slow tubs are cleared right there without any tools.

2.

What you need.

  • A small hand auger with a cable of 15 to 25 feet.
  • Rubber gloves and old towels.
  • A flashlight and a bucket.
  • A screwdriver for the stopper or overflow plate.
  • Hot water for flushing afterward.

Avoid a power drill on the snake in a tub, since it can scratch fixtures or kink the cable.

3.

Step by step.

  1. Remove the stopper or strainer. Clean off visible hair.
  2. Feed the cable in. Push it into the drain until you feel resistance.
  3. Turn the handle clockwise. Crank as you push, so the tip bites into the clog.
  4. Pull the clog out. Retract slowly and clean the cable with a rag.
  5. Repeat if needed. Run the cable several times until it passes freely.
  6. Flush with hot water. Run it for a few minutes to wash out residue.

If the cable will not go past the trap, the clog is beyond a tub snake's reach, or the trap is deeper than you think. Stop, since forcing it can scratch the tub or kink the cable.

4.

Using the overflow.

Some tubs have a mechanism inside the overflow that blocks the way. Remove the overflow plate and linkage, and feed the snake down through the overflow opening. This route often reaches farther and avoids the trap bend that stops a cable at the drain.

Reinstall the linkage carefully and test the stopper before you finish.

5.

When snaking is not enough.

If the tub drains and then returns to slow within days, or if other fixtures are slow as well, the clog is in the branch or main. Gurgling from a nearby toilet when the tub drains is a sign. At that point, a larger machine or a camera is needed. Our drain cleaning team uses cable machines and cameras to clear and inspect the line.

Do not pour chemical cleaner into a tub that is full of standing water. The chemical sits, damages finishes and splashes when you plunge. For a stubborn clog, call. See also hydro jetting for lines that keep returning.

6.

Mistakes that damage the tub or the pipe.

Forcing the cable is the usual one. A cable that kinks in the trap can get stuck, and pulling it out takes longer than clearing the clog would have. If the cable resists, back it out a few inches and rotate it, then try again. Never use a drill unless the snake is rated for it.

Another is skipping the cleanup. A clog pulled out and left in the tub bottom is a clog waiting to be flushed back down. Put the mess in the trash, not in the drain. And wipe down the cable before you coil it, or the next use will be unpleasant.

7.

Keeping the tub drain clear afterward.

  • Fit a hair catcher over the drain and empty it after showers.
  • Flush the drain with hot water once a week.
  • Clean the stopper every month or two.
  • Avoid washing pet hair or heavy debris down the tub.
  • Treat a slow tub quickly, before the clog hardens.

A hair catcher costs a few dollars and prevents most tub clogs. It is the single best habit in a house with long hair.

8.

Choosing a snake and when a bigger tool pays off.

A basic hand auger costs little and clears tub clogs in the trap and the first section of branch line. A drum machine with a longer cable reaches deeper and clears more stubborn mats. A cable machine with a cutting head handles roots and heavy buildup. The longer and thicker the cable, the more skill it takes to use without kinking or scratching pipe.

If you have tried a hand snake twice and the tub is slow again within a week, the cause is beyond its reach. The next step is a camera to see what is in the line. We run that visit as a routine call, and it often costs less than repeated attempts and the water they waste.

9.

What the clog tells you about your plumbing.

What comes out on the cable is a clue. A black, slimy mat of hair and soap is a routine tub clog. Fine gray sludge suggests soap scum and body oils that have built up on the pipe wall. Chunks of rust or flaking metal point to an aging cast iron or galvanized drain. Roots mean a break in the line outside.

Keep a sample of anything unusual and show it to the plumber. A plumber who sees rust flakes will want to look at the pipe with a camera, and one who sees roots will know to check the main. That small detail can save a second visit.

10.

Local note for Concord homes.

Local note.

Soft water and soap scum.

Concord's treated water measured about 12 parts per million of hardness in the city's 2022 report. Soft water lathers heavily and rinses slowly, so soap and shampoo residue sticks to hair and builds up in tub drains. Hard-water towns see mineral scale. Here, the clog is more often a soap and hair mat.

Older Concord homes can also have a tub trap in cast iron or lead, and a rough interior surface that catches debris. If you rent in a multi-unit building, the shared stack may be where the clog is. Ask the landlord to call us, and see our Hopkinton and Pembroke pages for other towns we serve.
