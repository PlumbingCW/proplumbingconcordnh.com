Emergency Leak Detection in Concord, NH.

Water coming through a ceiling or a meter that will not stop spinning is urgent, so we run leak detection calls 24/7 in Concord. Shut off the main valve if water is actively flowing, move belongings out from under any stain and avoid switching on lights or fans in a room with a wet ceiling.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

A running meter or a high water bill.

The quickest test is at the meter. Turn off every fixture, then watch the low-flow indicator, usually a small triangle or dial. If it moves, water is going somewhere. Check the toilets first, because a silent flapper leak is the most common cause.

The city says a typical household loses more than 10,000 gallons a year to leaks, and a showerhead dripping 10 times a minute wastes over 500 gallons a year. Older toilets that use up to 6 gallons a flush, compared with 1.3 for a WaterSense model, can add to a bill without any leak at all.

To test a toilet, drop dye or a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing and look for color in the bowl. If the meter still moves with every fixture off, the leak is probably in a pipe.

2.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks.

A brown ring on a ceiling that grows after a shower is a supply or drain leak from above, not a roof leak. A stain that grows only when it rains points to the roof. We use a moisture meter and a thermal camera to follow water from the stain to its source, often some distance away.

If the ceiling is bulging, poke a small hole to let the water drain into a bucket before it collapses. That relieves weight and can save the whole ceiling. Then call us.

3.

Slab leaks and warm spots in the floor.

A warm patch on a floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, or a sudden jump in the water bill can point to a leak in a pipe under a concrete slab. Hot water lines are the common culprit. We use acoustic listening gear and a thermal camera to pin the location to a few square feet.

Repair options for a slab leak are to break the slab and fix the pipe, reroute the line through walls or attic, or in some cases repipe. We lay out each with its trade-offs, and the choice is yours.

4.

Under the house, crawlspace and buried lines.

Crawlspace leaks hide behind insulation and plastic and show up as musty smells, sagging insulation and mold on joists. A leak in the buried water line between the street and the house shows up as low pressure, a soft wet patch in the yard or a meter that runs with the house shut off.

Buried-line leaks are the ones where detection pays off most. Pinpointing the spot lets us dig a small hole rather than a long trench. Call 811 before any digging, which is free and required in New Hampshire. Our water service line page covers repair and replacement.

6.

Irrigation, yard and meter box leaks.

Irrigation systems leak from broken heads, cracked valves and winterization problems. If the meter runs only when the sprinkler zone is off, a valve is probably stuck open. A leak in the meter pit shows as water standing around the lid.

Pool and spa leaks are a specialty of their own. We can test the plumbing lines serving a pool, but we do not repair pool shells or liners.

7.

Commercial building leaks.

A leak in a small business or apartment building raises questions beyond repair: who is responsible, and how do you keep the business open? We locate the leak, isolate the affected section so water stays on elsewhere, and document it for the property manager. See our commercial plumbing page for building-wide work.
