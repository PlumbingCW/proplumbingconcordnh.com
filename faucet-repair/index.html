Emergency Faucet Repair in Concord, NH.

A faucet or valve that will not shut off can flood a kitchen or bathroom, so our line is open 24/7 for fixture emergencies in Concord. Close the angle stop under the sink or, if it is stuck, the main water valve, and put a bucket under any drip.

1.

When a faucet or valve will not shut off.

A stuck shutoff valve is the part that turns a dripping faucet into an emergency. Old angle stops under sinks seize from mineral buildup or corrosion and will not turn, or they break when you force them. If a supply line bursts and the stop will not close, the main valve is your fallback.

A tub or shower valve that will not stop is a different problem, because the cartridge or valve body has failed inside the wall. Do not keep forcing the handle. Shut the main and call us.

2.

Common faucet problems and fixes.

A single-handle cartridge faucet is usually a ten minute repair once we have the right cartridge. Older compression faucets need new washers and seats, and sometimes the whole fixture is cheaper to replace.

3.

Tub and shower valves, showerheads and bath faucets.

Water behind a tub wall is the problem to fear. A shower valve that drips even after the handle is off, or a bathtub faucet that sprays from the diverter, can leak inside the wall. We replace the cartridge, the stem or the valve body, and open the wall only when we must.

Showerheads that dribble are usually clogged with debris. In Concord's soft water, mineral crusts are less of a problem than they are in hard-water towns, so a quick clean often works. A shower that leaks behind the tile or a stain on the ceiling below needs a leak detection visit.

4.

Repair or replace the fixture.

If the faucet is old, pitted or no longer has parts available, replacement is cheaper than repair. If you like the faucet, we repair it. If you want a new one, we install it, and if you buy it we check that it fits your sink holes and supply lines.

  • Age and finish. Old chrome pits and flakes, and replacement parts become scarce.
  • Valve type. Cartridge faucets repair easily, compression faucets need regular washers.
  • Brand and parts. Some brands have long parts availability, others do not.
  • What is behind it. Corroded shutoffs and supply lines are often replaced at the same visit.
5.

Burst or leaking supply valves.

A supply valve or flexible line that bursts under a sink dumps water quickly, often while no one is home. Braided stainless lines are better than the old plastic ones, and replacing them every ten years or so is cheap insurance. We swap old lines and stops during the same visit as a faucet repair.

If a pipe feeding the valve is itself leaking, see our burst pipe repair page.

  • 3.Put towels and a bucket under the leak.
  • 4.Open the faucet to drain the line and relieve pressure.
  • 5.Take a photo of the fixture and any part numbers.
