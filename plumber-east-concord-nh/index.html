Plumber in East Concord, Concord, NH.

East Concord lies east of the river, north of I-393, and its streets are more spread out than the rest of the city. Before any plumbing work, the first question here is whether your home is on city sewer or a septic system, and whether it uses city water or a well. For the rest of the city, see our Concord, NH plumbing page.

ZIP codes we cover in and around East Concord0330103303
1.

City service or private system.

The Master Plan describes East Concord as a mixed area of small manufacturing along Mill Brook, farms and summer camps, with simple worker houses and farm and merchant homes. A real estate guide describes detached houses, condominiums and neighborhood streets in lower-density settings, with topography and wetlands worth checking.

We cannot tell from the neighborhood name whether a given house has city sewer or a septic tank. The City says its collection system has 163 miles of sanitary sewer line. If you are unsure which applies to you, the City's General Services at 603-228-2737 can confirm for your address.

2.

If your house is on a septic system.

A septic backup is not a main line clog. Cabling a house drain does not fix a full tank or a failing leach field, and the backup comes back. If more than one fixture is slow and you have a septic system, stop running water and call. Tell us when the tank was last pumped. Our drain cleaning page explains what we can clear, and leak detection helps when a wet patch shows up in the yard.

3.

If your house is on a private well.

The state environmental agency says radon and arsenic are the contaminants most often found in New Hampshire private wells, and recommends testing at an accredited lab every three to five years. If your home has a well, a loss of pressure can be the pump, the pressure switch or the tank, not the plumbing in the house. If the lights are out, the pump has no power.

4.

Streets and routes.

The Master Plan names Eastman, Portsmouth and Shawmut Streets and Mountain Road among East Concord's historic streets, and Graham, Snow Pond and Hot Hole Pond Roads in its rural part. Routes lead to I-93 and toward Loudon. Our Loudon page covers the towns just beyond.

5.

Permits and inspections in Concord.

Whether a job needs a permit depends on the work. The City's Building Safety Division at 41 Green Street handles permits, and the licensed plumbing contractor schedules inspections with the City's Plumbing and Mechanical Inspector, usually on site. The division's number is 603-225-8610. If you are connecting to city sewer or adding a new service line, ask the City before work starts.

6.

Get ready for the call.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

  • 1.Find the main shutoff, and the pump breaker if you have a well.
  • 2.Shut off the water at the main if anything is flowing.
  • 3.Stop using drains if you have a septic system and it is backing up.
  • 4.Note whether you are on city sewer or septic, and city water or a well.
  • 5.Have your last septic pumping or water test records ready.
