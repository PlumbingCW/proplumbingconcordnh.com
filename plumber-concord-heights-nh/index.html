Plumber in Concord Heights, Concord, NH.

Concord Heights is the plateau near the airport and the river bluffs. It mixes residential streets with shopping and car-oriented businesses, so a plumbing call here can be for a house or a small business. Tell us which when you call. For the rest of the city, see our Concord, NH plumbing page.

1.

A plateau with homes and businesses.

The Master Plan describes Concord Heights as a plateau near the airport and river bluffs, with auto-oriented commercial use, the airport and high-density residential areas. Residential growth began in the 1920s and continued with post-World War II subdivisions. Loudon Road and Manchester Street are its main routes.

That mix means two kinds of calls. We treat a house and a small business differently, so tell us which one you are calling about.

2.

Mid-century homes and small additions.

Subdivision homes built after the war are often modest in size and have been changed by several owners. We do not guess what a particular house contains. We look for the shutoffs, the age of the water heater and the way the drains are routed, and then recommend the smallest repair that works.

If one fixture drains slowly while the others are fine, the problem is usually local to that fixture. If several are slow, the main line is the likely cause. Drain cleaning explains both cases.

3.

Shops, restaurants and rentals.

Near the commercial parts of Loudon Road and Manchester Street, small businesses have different needs. A restaurant may need grease control and a floor drain checked. A shop may need a toilet or a water heater repaired fast. Commercial plumbing describes how we handle those, and hydro jetting covers scouring a grease-prone line.

4.

Basements and wet floors.

If water shows up in a basement, check whether it comes from a pipe, a drain or the ground. Water that appears after rain points to the ground, and a sump pump may be involved. Water that appears with no rain, or near the water heater or a laundry line, points to a pipe. Tell us what you saw and when.

5.

Permits and inspections in Concord.

Whether a job needs a permit depends on the work. The City's Building Safety Division at 41 Green Street handles permits, and the licensed plumbing contractor schedules inspections with the City's Plumbing and Mechanical Inspector, usually on site. The division's number is 603-225-8610. For a business, ask the City whether the work changes anything about the building's approvals.

Get ready for the call.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

  • 1.Shut off the water at the main if anything is flowing.
  • 2.Switch off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • 3.Move stock, furniture or belongings away from the water.
  • 4.Note whether it is a home or a business.
  • 5.Photograph the problem.
