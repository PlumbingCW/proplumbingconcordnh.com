Whole House Repiping in Concord, NH.

Repiping replaces the water supply pipes inside a house, and it is worth doing when old galvanized steel, failing copper or repeated pinhole leaks keep costing you repairs. We plan repipes in Concord to keep walls and floors intact and the water off for as short a time as possible.

If a pipe is leaking right now, start with burst pipe repair.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

Signs your pipes are due.

Repeated leaks in different spots are the clearest sign. Others are rusty or brown water when you first turn on a tap, low pressure throughout the house, discolored water that returns after flushing and visible green corrosion on copper or flaking on steel.

Galvanized steel pipe was common in older houses and corrodes from the inside, building up rust that narrows the bore. Pressure drops, water turns brown and eventually pinholes open. Houses with galvanized lines are the most common repipe we see.

2.

PEX, copper or CPVC.

Pipe material choices.
MaterialStrengthsWeaknesses.
PEXFlexible, fewer joints, tolerates freezing betterNot for outdoor use in sunlight, some prefer copper.
CopperLong lifespan, familiar, durableCosts more, can pinhole in aggressive water.
CPVCInexpensive, easy to joinCan become brittle and crack over time.

Most repipes today use PEX because it threads through walls with fewer openings, which keeps the drywall repair small. Copper is a good choice when you want a traditional material and the budget allows. We explain which fits your house.

3.

How we repipe a house.

  1. Walk the house. We map the fixtures, the main shutoff and where the old pipes run.
  2. Plan the route. New lines run through basements, crawlspaces and attics where we can, to avoid opening finished walls.
  3. Shut off and replace. Water is off for the working day, and we restore service to key fixtures each evening where possible.
  4. Pressure test. We test the new system before closing any wall.
  5. Patch access holes. Holes are kept small, and we leave them ready for a drywall contractor.

Most single-family homes take a few days. Larger homes and multi-unit buildings take longer. We give you a schedule before starting.

4.

Water quality and the pipe in your wall.

Concord's 2022 water quality report shows copper at 0.044 parts per million at the 90th percentile and lead at 5 parts per billion, both below action levels. The city adds corrosion control, with a distribution pH averaging about 9.2, to reduce metal leaching from plumbing.

That said, lead enters water mainly from service lines and home plumbing, not from the treated supply. If your house has old lead solder, galvanized fittings or a lead service line, repiping and replacing the service together makes sense. The city advises flushing cold water for 30 seconds after it has sat for hours.

5.

What drives the price.

  • House size and fixtures. More bathrooms, kitchens and outdoor spigots mean more pipe and more connections.
  • Access. An unfinished basement is quicker than a slab house with finished walls.
  • Material. PEX is generally less costly than copper.
  • Service line. Adding a new service line to the street increases the job.
  • Patching. Drywall and paint repair is separate from the plumbing.

A repipe is a large job, and we show you the price before work starts, with options if there is a phased path.

6.

Repipe or keep repairing.

If the pipes leak once in a few years, repair makes sense. If you have had three or more leaks, the walls are opened over and over, and water damage adds up. At that point a repipe usually costs less than the cumulative repairs.

A repipe is also a chance to move plumbing out of exterior walls and unheated spaces, which protects against the winter freezes we see around Concord, where frost reaches 48 inches. It also helps when you are planning a remodel, so we coordinate with plumbing installation work.

Before you schedule a repipe.

  • 1.Photograph any leaks, stains and the pipe you can see.
  • 2.Locate the main shutoff and confirm it closes fully.
  • 3.Decide which rooms must keep water longest during the job.
  • 4.Move belongings away from walls and the basement ceiling.
  • 5.Ask whether the work needs a permit.
