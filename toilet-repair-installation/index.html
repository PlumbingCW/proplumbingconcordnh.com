Emergency Toilet Repair in Concord, NH.

A toilet that is overflowing or leaking at the base is an emergency, and our line is open 24/7 for toilet calls in Concord. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water, lift the tank lid to hold the flapper closed if it is running and keep the floor dry.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

Overflowing or leaking: stop the water first.

The shutoff valve is a small oval or round handle on the wall or floor behind the toilet. If it will not turn or does not stop the flow, close the main water valve for the house. An overflowing bowl that does not clear after one plunge is likely a deeper blockage, and plunging repeatedly only spreads the mess.

Water at the base of a toilet means the wax ring or the closet bolts have failed, or the flange is cracked. Sewer gas, a rocking toilet and a soft floor all point the same way. It needs a plumber before the subfloor rots.

2.

A toilet that runs, hisses or flushes weakly.

Common toilet faults.
SymptomLikely causeFix.
Runs on and offWorn flapper or chainReplace flapper.

The city notes that an older toilet can use up to 6 gallons a flush, while a WaterSense model uses 1.3. A silent leak around the flapper wastes water and can show up as a high bill. We tell you how to run a dye test to check.

3.

Flange repair.

The flange is the ring that bolts the toilet to the floor and connects it to the drain. When it cracks or rusts, the toilet rocks and leaks. A repair ring or a full flange replacement fixes it, and in older houses we check the subfloor for rot at the same time.

A toilet that gurgles or backs up when other fixtures run may have a vent or main line problem rather than a toilet problem. Our sewer line cleaning page describes how we tell.

4.

Replacing or installing a toilet.

A new toilet is a planned job once you choose a model. We measure the rough-in, which is the distance from the wall to the drain center, and match a toilet that fits. We remove the old unit, set a new wax ring and flange hardware, connect a new supply line and shutoff and test for leaks.

Comfort-height, dual-flush and one-piece models are all options. A water-efficient model also lowers usage, which matters for homes on private wells and septic in Bow and Loudon, where every gallon goes through your own system.

5.

When the clog is not in the toilet.

If a plunger and a closet auger do not clear it, the blockage is deeper. We may pull the toilet to access the drain, or camera the line. Never flush wipes, toys or paper towels, which cause many of the toilet calls we see.

What to do while you wait.

  • 1.Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet.
  • 2.Do not flush again.
  • 3.Put towels around the base.
  • 4.Keep the lid up if it is overflowing from the tank.
  • 5.Tell us if other fixtures are also slow.
6.

What drives the price.

  • Parts or whole toilet. A flapper or fill valve is a small repair, a new toilet is a larger one.
  • Flange and floor condition. A rotted subfloor under the toilet adds carpentry.
  • Clog depth. A blockage in the toilet trap is quick, and one in the main line needs more equipment.
  • Timing. After-hours work can be priced differently from a daytime visit.

We give you the price before work starts, and if we find a second problem while we are there, we tell you before touching it.
