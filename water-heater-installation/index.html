Water Heater Installation in Concord, NH.

Installing a water heater is a planned job when it is done right, and it is the best time to fix the details that shorten a tank's life. We handle new installs for remodels, additions and fuel changes in Concord, with same-day appointments when available.

If your current heater has already failed, our emergency replacement page covers the 24/7 route.

1.

What a new installation involves.

A water heater installation is more than setting a tank in place. It includes supply and return piping, a shutoff valve, a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge line, a drain pan where required, venting for gas units and a dedicated circuit for electric ones. Each of these has a code requirement, and skipping one is what separates a quick job from a good one.

We confirm the fuel, the available space and the venting path before quoting. A heater in a closet needs combustion air, and one in a crawlspace needs clearance for service.

2.

A tank that is too small runs out mid-shower. One that is too large wastes energy keeping water hot. We ask about your morning routine and not just the square footage.

3.

Tank, hybrid or tankless.

A standard tank is the lowest cost and the simplest. A hybrid heat pump unit cuts electric use sharply but needs space and a drain. Tankless units provide endless hot water and save floor space, but need larger gas lines or electrical service and can cost much more to install.

In New Hampshire, incoming water is cold for much of the year, which affects how hard a tankless unit has to work. We size the unit for winter inlet temperatures, not summer ones.

4.

Expansion tanks, valves and the details.

When a house has a backflow preventer or pressure-reducing valve on the incoming line, heated water has nowhere to expand. Pressure spikes can trip the relief valve and shorten the tank's life. An expansion tank sized to the system absorbs the swing.

We also fit a new shutoff valve, flexible connectors with dielectric protection where copper meets steel, and a discharge line that ends at a safe point. Seismic-style strapping is added where local requirements apply.

5.

Venting and fuel safety.

A gas water heater must vent combustion gases outside, through a flue or a direct vent. A flue that is too short, sloped wrong or blocked can let carbon monoxide into the house. We test draft and combustion after installation and recommend a carbon monoxide detector near sleeping areas.

If you smell gas at any time, leave the house, call 911 or your gas utility and do not use switches or phones inside. We can help with the plumbing side afterward.

6.

Permits and inspection in Concord.

Where a permit is required, the licensed plumbing contractor arranges the inspection with the city's plumbing inspector. Concord's Building Safety Division is at 41 Green Street, and we handle the scheduling. Ask us up front whether your job needs one.

Before the installer arrives.

  • 1.Clear a 3 foot path to the heater.
  • 2.Note the current fuel, size and age.
  • 3.Tell us about any changes you want, such as moving the heater.
  • 4.Check that you have a working carbon monoxide detector.
  • 5.Have the electrical panel accessible for an electric unit.
