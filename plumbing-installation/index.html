Plumbing Installation in Concord, NH.

New plumbing goes in during remodels, additions, basement finishes and fixture upgrades, and the quality of the rough-in decides how it performs for decades. We install supply and drain lines, fixtures, laundry hookups, water treatment and pumps for homes and small properties in Concord.

1.

What we install.

Common jobs include new bathrooms, kitchen sink and dishwasher hookups, laundry and utility sinks, outdoor spigots, basement bathrooms, water treatment equipment and pumps. We also add shutoff valves where a house has none, which pays off the first time you have a leak.

For a new bath or kitchen, we coordinate the rough-in with the contractor, test the lines under pressure before walls close and return for finish work when fixtures are ready.

2.

Basement bathrooms and below-grade drains.

A bathroom below the main sewer level cannot drain by gravity. It needs a sewage ejector pump in a sealed basin, or a below-floor drain cut through the slab to a lower connection. Which is right depends on the depth of the sewer and the layout of the house.

A sump pump is a different device. It moves groundwater from a pit to the outside to keep a basement dry. A sump pump and an ejector pump are often confused, but one handles groundwater and the other handles sewage. We install either, and the right choice is not always obvious.

3.

Do you need a water softener in Concord?.

Often not. The city's treated water averages about 12 parts per million of hardness in its 2022 report, which is soft. Softeners remove hardness, so in city water they have little to remove. We would sooner tell you that than sell you one.

On a private well, the picture changes. Wells in nearby towns can carry hardness, iron or manganese, and the state's environmental agency recommends testing well water at an accredited lab every few years. If your test shows a problem, we can install the right treatment. Our Bow page covers the well testing data for that town.

4.

Gas piping, safety first.

If you smell gas, leave the building, then call 911 or your gas utility from outside, before you call us. Do not flip switches or use a phone indoors. Gas problems are for the utility first, and we help with plumbing after it is safe.

For planned installs, we connect and pressure-test gas piping that serves plumbing appliances such as water heaters. We tell you when a gas job belongs with the utility or another trade.

5.

New construction and additions.

A new build or addition needs a rough-in plan: drain slopes, vents, supply layout and the point where it ties into the existing house. Mistakes buried in a wall are costly. We review plans with the builder and flag conflicts early.

Concord's Building Safety Division at 41 Green Street handles permits, and the licensed contractor schedules plumbing inspections with the city's inspector. We take care of that step, so you do not have to. For a house with failing pipes, repiping is often the first project.

7.

Looking after pumps once they are in.

A sump pump needs a test twice a year. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float lift and the pump discharge. A pump that runs but does not move water has a stuck check valve or a clogged impeller, and spring thaw in New Hampshire is a bad time to find that out.

Ejector pumps handle sewage and should be on their own circuit with a sealed basin lid. If the alarm sounds, stop using the basement bathroom and call us. Pumps that serve a wet basement are the ones most often called in on a stormy night, so a battery backup is worth considering.
