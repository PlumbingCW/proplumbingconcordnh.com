1.

A water system in transition.

Pembroke's public water has been in the news. Pembroke Water Works serves part of the town, and PFAS were found in the supply. In February 2025 the Concord Monitor reported a proposed $10.4 million bond to connect to Concord's water. Concord's Sanders Station wells already sit beside the Soucook River in Pembroke and serve as an emergency source for Concord.

We do not test water, and we cannot tell you what is in yours. If you want a filter, the right place to start is your water supplier's results and a lab report. A plumber can install a filter at the point of entry or at a sink, and we explain the choices without selling more than you need. See plumbing installation.

2.

Suncook Village and North Pembroke.

Pembroke's two main villages are North Pembroke and Suncook Village, and Pembroke addresses carry the Suncook post office name with the 03275 ZIP. Suncook Village sits along the Merrimack and the Soucook, with older homes close together. North Pembroke is quieter and more spread out.

The town has a municipal wastewater plant, and many homes use private septic. In a village home on sewer, backups usually come from a lateral clog. In a septic home, the cause may be the tank. Our sewer inspection page describes how a camera tells them apart.

4.

Getting here.

US 3, US 4 and US 202 cross Pembroke, with NH 9, NH 28 and NH 106 close by. I-93 Exit 13 is about five miles away. The river and bridges between Pembroke and Concord can back up in rush hour or a storm, and we tell you honestly what the schedule looks like.

For jobs near the Concord line, see Bow or water heater replacement if a tank has failed.

5.

Older homes by the river.

Homes near the river can have damp basements, sump pumps and floor drains that take on water. A pump that fails during spring thaw is a classic call. We install and repair sump and ejector pumps, and we recommend a battery backup for any basement that has flooded.

A floor drain with a dry trap lets sewer gas up. Pour water down it every few weeks.

6.

Ready for a Pembroke visit.

  • Know your water. Pembroke Water Works, a private well or another supplier.
  • Know your sewer. Town sewer or septic.
  • Locate the shutoffs. Main and water heater.
  • Move belongings. Away from the basement floor.
  • Note the symptoms. Water color, smell and noise.
  • Have your address ready. Suncook and Pembroke addresses can be confusing.
7.

Spring thaw and the Soucook.

Pembroke sits between two rivers, the Merrimack and the Soucook, and the water table rises fast when snow melts or a storm hits. A basement that stays dry in August can take on water in April. If a sump pump runs constantly or a floor drain gurgles, call before the pit overflows.

Backup power matters too. A pump without electricity does nothing, and storms often knock out power at the worst time. A battery backup or a water-powered unit keeps a sump going. We explain which fits your pit and your budget.
