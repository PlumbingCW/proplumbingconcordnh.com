Plumbing Service Areas Near Concord, NH.

We take calls for Concord and five nearby towns. Each town has its own water and sewer setup, so each has its own page.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

Nearby towns we serve.

Bow, NH.

Mostly private wells and septic. Well pressure tanks, treatment and winter calls.

Hopkinton, NH.

A large western town of villages, precinct water and private systems.

Hooksett, NH.

I-93 corridor, precinct water, sewer billing and small commercial calls.

Pembroke, NH.

Suncook and North Pembroke, a changing public water system and river-side basements.

Loudon, NH.

Rural wells and septic with no municipal sewer.

2.

Inside Concord.

In the city, we work in Downtown, the North End, the South End, the West End, East Concord, West Concord, Concord Heights and Penacook. Concord has the ZIP codes 03301 and 03303 for street addresses, 03302 for post office boxes and 03305 for the state's Department of Safety.

Concord is served by city water and the city's wastewater system. For where the city's responsibility ends and yours begins, General Services at 603-228-2737 can confirm for your address. Our services page lists everything we do in the city.

3.

Concord neighborhoods.

Penacook.

Former mill village at the north edge, partly in Boscawen.

West Concord.

Granite and farming village with many early houses.

East Concord.

Lower-density area where city and private systems both appear.

South End.

Historic residential streets with single and multifamily homes.

West End.

Dense mid-1800s streets and large Colonial Revival houses.

Concord Heights.

Post-war homes next to shops and the airport.

4.

How water and sewer differ across our area.

The biggest difference between towns is where the water comes from and where the wastewater goes. That changes what we bring to a job and what we ask when you call.

5.

Which page to read first.

If you live in a house on a private well, start with the Bow page or the Loudon page. Both explain how a pressure tank, switch and septic system change an emergency call. If you pay a sewer bill and have precinct water, the Hooksett page explains how that billing works and when a well meter helps.

If you live in a village with older homes, the Hopkinton page covers shared stacks and older piping. If your supply is changing, the Pembroke page covers the water system questions and the spring thaw issues near the rivers.

6.

Towns near the edge of our area.

Chichester, Epsom, Allenstown, Henniker and Webster sit next to the towns above. We do not have separate pages for them, but call and give us the address. We will tell you honestly whether we can reach you.

Each town page explains whether homes there are mostly on wells or town water, and what that means for a plumbing call.

7.

What to expect when we come to your town.

  1. We confirm the address. Town, street and whether you are on a well or town water.
  2. We give safe steps. Shutoffs first, then what to leave alone.
  3. The plumber diagnoses on site. You hear the cause and the price before work starts.
  4. We repair and test. Pressure, drainage and a check for leaks before we leave.

Travel time varies with weather, traffic and the time of day, so we tell you honestly what the schedule looks like and keep you posted if it changes. Winter storms on I-93, I-89 and the state routes between towns can slow a truck, and we plan around them.
