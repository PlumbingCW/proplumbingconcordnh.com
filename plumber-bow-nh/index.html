1.

No water at the tap: a well, pressure tank or pipe problem.

Most Bow homes do not connect to a public water main. Town information puts roughly 90 percent of residents on private wells, and the municipal system that began serving in July 2012 reaches only the commercial and industrial zone along Dunklee Road, Johnson Road, River Road, Route 3A and Vaughn Road. That means a sudden loss of water at a Bow house is often a well-side problem, not a city main break.

A quick check helps. If the lights are out, the pump has no power. If power is on and the tank gauge reads zero, the pressure switch, pressure tank or pump may have failed. If pressure is low only on hot water, the cause is in the water heater. We handle the plumbing side, from pressure tanks and switches to the lines and fixtures, and we tell you plainly when a well contractor needs to pull the pump.

2.

What Bow well water does to plumbing.

The state's environmental agency says radon and arsenic are the contaminants most often found in New Hampshire private wells. Bow's own 2015 to 2019 private well samples found arsenic above 5 parts per billion in 59 percent of tests. Radon topped 4,000 picocuries per liter in 55 percent, manganese topped 0.05 parts per million in 26 percent and uranium topped 30 parts per billion in 7 percent.

Manganese and iron stain fixtures and clog aerators, and minerals shorten the life of a water heater. The state recommends testing well water every three to five years at an accredited lab. If your results call for a filter or softener, our plumbing installation team can install treatment matched to the test, and we will not sell you equipment for a problem your results do not show.

3.

Septic and sewer in Bow.

Homes outside the commercial sewer area use private septic. A septic backup is not a main line clog, and cabling the house line does not fix a full tank or a failing leach field. When a toilet or tub backs up in a septic home, stop using water, skip the chemical cleaners and call. We will tell you whether the blockage is in the house line, which we clear, or at the tank, which needs a septic specialist.

If a property is on town sewer or planning to connect, the town requires a Plumbing and Sewer Permit through the Community Development Building Permits office. Town hall is at 10 Grandview Road. See sewer line cleaning for how we clear sewered homes.

4.

Winter calls on rural lots.

Long supply runs between a well, a basement and a house are exposed to freezing. Pipes in unheated crawlspaces, well houses and exterior walls split in hard cold, and a break in a line to the well house can drain the pressure tank. For active leaks see our burst pipe repair page, and for a leak whose location you cannot see, leak detection is the first step.

A heated well house or insulation on the line is the cheap fix. We can add heat tape and insulation while we repair the damaged section.

6.

Get your Bow home ready for a plumber.

  • Know your water source. Tell us whether you are on a well or town water, and where the pressure tank is.
  • Find the shutoffs. The main valve and, on a well, the pump breaker.
  • Gather your last water test. If you have a lab report, keep it handy.
  • Clear the path. To the basement, tank and water heater.
  • Note symptoms. No water, low pressure, sputtering faucets or discolored water.
  • Keep your phone on. We call before we arrive.
