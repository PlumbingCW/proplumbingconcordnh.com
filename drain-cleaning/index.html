Emergency Drain Cleaning in Concord, NH.

Water rising in a tub or sewage coming up a floor drain needs attention now, so we take drain calls 24/7 in Concord. While you wait, stop running water anywhere in the house, keep the toilet lid closed and move rugs and boxes off the floor near the drain.

1.

Sewer backup: what to do first.

If more than one fixture is slow or gurgling at once, the blockage is likely in the main line rather than a single trap. A toilet bubbling when you run the washer, or a basement floor drain taking on water after a shower upstairs, are classic main line signs.

Stop using water, because every gallon you send down has nowhere to go. Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line. It sits in standing water, burns skin if it splashes and can damage pipe and make our work harder. Call us instead and we will arrive prepared for a main line job.

2.

Slow sink, tub or kitchen drain.

A single slow fixture is almost always a local clog. In a kitchen, it is grease and food that cooled and hardened in the trap and the first few feet of pipe. In a bathroom it is hair bound with soap, which forms a rope just below the stopper.

We clear these with a hand auger or a small drum machine, then flush the line with hot water and check the flow. If a sink drains but stays slow, we run a camera to see whether the pipe is scaled, offset or collapsing.

Some slow drains are not a clog at all. A blocked vent stack can make a sink gulp and drain slowly, which is something our toilet repair visits also uncover.

4.

Floor drains and drain line leaks.

Basement floor drains in older Concord homes often tie into the main drain with a trap that dries out and lets sewer gas up. Pouring a quart of water into the drain every few weeks keeps the trap sealed.

A drain line that leaks inside a wall or under a slab sounds different from a clog. You may see a stain that grows after use, hear dripping when a fixture drains, or smell sewage with no visible backup. We locate the leak with a camera and, if needed, leak detection gear before opening anything.

5.

Why Concord drains clog the way they do.

Concord's water is very soft. Soft water leaves little mineral scale in a pipe, but soap lathers heavily and sticks to hair and grease, so soap scum buildup in bathroom drains is common. Older homes with cast iron or clay lines add roughness where debris catches.

Tree roots are the other repeat offender, especially in the older neighborhoods with mature maples and a clay or cast iron line to the street. Roots enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper. A root cut clears the line for a season or two, and a repair or liner is the permanent answer.

Private septic systems in Bow and Loudon behave differently from city sewer, and we cover those on the Loudon page.

6.

What a drain visit looks like.

  1. Locate the blockage. We test fixtures and find the cleanout to decide where the clog sits.
  2. Clear it. The right tool goes in based on the pipe size, material and what is likely causing the clog.
  3. Confirm flow. We run water at every affected fixture and check that it drains steadily.
  4. Camera check if needed. A recurring clog earns a look inside the pipe before we leave.
  5. Tell you what we saw. You hear the cause and whether it is likely to return.

You get a clear price before work starts, and we do not push a bigger job than the problem needs.
