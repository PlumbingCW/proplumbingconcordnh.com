Plumber in West End, Concord, NH.

The West End is a residential neighborhood west of the State House and downtown. It covers closely built streets of mid-19th-century houses and, further west, large Colonial Revival houses on bigger lots. Big, old houses have a lot of fixtures, so many plumbing calls here are about hot water, valves and small leaks. For the rest of the city, see our Concord, NH plumbing page.

1.

Lower West End and Upper West End.

The Master Plan separates the Lower West End, around Centre, Pleasant, Merrimack, Warren and West Washington Streets, from the Upper West End, west of Liberty Street, around School, Auburn and the lower end of Ridge Road. It describes the lower area as dense with uniform lots and mid-19th-century houses, and the upper area as large lots with Colonial Revival houses built about 1890 to 1910.

Real estate sources add that the area has access to Concord Hospital, downtown and I-89. We go by your street address.

2.

Large houses, many fixtures.

A house with several bathrooms, a kitchen and a laundry draws a lot of hot water at once. When the tank runs out early, the heater is often undersized for how the house is used. Tell us how many people live in the house and how many bathrooms you run in the morning. We use that to size a replacement or recommend a tankless model.

3.

Valves, supply lines and small leaks.

More fixtures means more shutoff valves and more supply lines, and the ones nobody opens are the ones that fail. If a valve weeps when turned, do not keep turning it. Shut the main and call. Leak detection explains how we find a hidden leak when a stain shows on a ceiling and the pipe is not visible.

If a house has been renovated in pieces, ask us about repiping only if leaks keep returning. A single leak is not a reason to replace the whole system.

4.

Close lots and mistaken blame.

The Master Plan describes the Lower West End as densely built on uniform lots. When houses sit that close together, a drain backup is easy to blame on a neighbor. A camera inspection shows whether the cause is your own line.

5.

Permits and inspections in Concord.

Whether a job needs a permit depends on the work. The City's Building Safety Division at 41 Green Street handles permits, and the licensed plumbing contractor schedules inspections with the City's Plumbing and Mechanical Inspector, usually on site. The division's number is 603-225-8610. A water heater replacement in a large house is the kind of job where it is worth asking first.

6.

Get ready for the call.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

  • 1.Shut off the water at the main if anything is flowing.
  • 2.Switch off power or gas to a leaking water heater.
  • 3.Note the water heater age if the tag is readable.
  • 4.Move belongings away from wet walls and floors.
  • 5.Photograph the leak and where it shows up.
