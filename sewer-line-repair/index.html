Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Concord, NH.

A cracked or collapsed sewer line can put raw sewage under a house, so our line is open 24/7 for sewer repair calls in Concord. Stop running water, keep everyone out of the area and call us to arrange a camera inspection that shows exactly what is wrong.

Call (603) 815-9179
1.

Signs the line is broken, not just clogged.

A clog clears when cleaned. A broken line comes back. Repeat backups after cleaning, sewage smell in the yard, a wet or sunken patch over the pipe path, sewer gas in the basement and slow drains throughout the house all suggest damage.

If you have an emergency, such as sewage on the floor, we will clear the line first to get the house usable and then camera it to see what failed. You do not have to decide on a repair while standing in a flooded basement.

2.

What a camera shows.

A sewer camera on a push cable shows cracks, offsets, roots, belly sags and collapsed sections, along with the depth and distance from the cleanout. A locator on the surface marks the spot so a repair can be targeted rather than guessed.

We show you the video and explain what you are seeing. For a standalone planned inspection, see our sewer inspection page.

3.

Repair options: dig, line or replace.

4.

Cast iron and clay pipe in older homes.

Cast iron pipe was standard in many older houses. It corrodes from the inside, forms rough scale, and eventually cracks or flakes through. Clay pipe has joints that roots find easily. Orangeburg, a tarred fiber pipe, sags and collapses with age.

For a cast iron pipe with a single crack, a repair sleeve or short section replacement can fix it. If the whole pipe has scaled and thinned, replacement is the lasting answer.

5.

Digging in Concord: frost, utilities and permits.

Concord's frost depth is 48 inches, so a lateral laid shallower than that, as some older ones are, can freeze in a hard winter. We check depth during the camera run.

Before any dig, we call 811, the free utility locating service required in New Hampshire. Work in the public right-of-way involves the city. The city's Building Safety Division at 41 Green Street handles permits, and plumbing inspections are scheduled by the licensed contractor, so that step is ours to manage.

6.

What drives the cost.

  • Depth and length. A deeper pipe or a longer run takes more time and equipment.
  • Access. A line under a driveway, patio or garage floor costs more to reach.
  • Method. Lining and bursting avoid some restoration costs, but the equipment is specialized.
  • Pipe material and condition. Cast iron and clay can need more repair than plastic.
  • Restoration. Landscaping, concrete and paving repair are separate from the plumbing.

We give you the price before work starts, with the options side by side.

7.

After the repair.

What to do while you wait for the repair.

  • 1.Stop running water in the house.
  • 2.Keep people and pets away from any sewage.
  • 3.Do not use chemical drain cleaners.
  • 4.Photograph the damage for your records.
  • 5.Wear gloves and boots if you must clean up spills.

When the new pipe or liner is in, we camera it again to confirm the repair holds and the line drains. You get the before and after video, and we note the depth and material for your records. If the repair involved an excavation, we backfill and compact the trench, and surface restoration such as landscaping or paving is handled separately.
